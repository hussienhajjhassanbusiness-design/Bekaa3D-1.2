# ADR-047: Account deletion by anonymisation - one transaction under a fixed lock order, every personal field stripped, records kept, requests queued behind it refused (VS-045)

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-28, VS-045).
**Date:** 2026-10-28
**Source basis:** [VS-045 slice](../vertical-slice-plan.md#vs-045--account-deletion-by-anonymisation); owner answers to plan questions Q1-Q10 of 2026-10-28 (Q1 and Q2 extended by the owner, the rest as recommended), and later owner decisions on addresses and on three review findings (phone hash, customer free text, chargeback window); SRS §14.5, BR-125, BR-126, F-035; FR-03; api-endpoints §9.1; [ADR-024](ADR-024-cash-on-delivery-and-courier-priced-shipping.md) d9 and its VS-045 note; [ADR-027](ADR-027-address-book-decisions.md); [ADR-038](ADR-038-physical-checkout-and-cash-eligibility.md); [ADR-041](ADR-041-receipt-confirmation-and-problem-report.md); [ADR-042](ADR-042-refunds-voids-and-chargebacks.md); [ADR-046](ADR-046-customer-data-export.md).
**Amends:** the slice's "Model / migration: None" (migration `0b349a492461`, d2 and d3) and its "lock the user, then re-check" (d6, ADR-041's order wins).
**Applies first to:** `DELETE /api/v1/me`; head `0b349a492461`.

## Context

SRS §14.5 keeps financial and delivery records "in anonymised form", and the slice's test says they remain "with no PII". The order snapshots and the address book hold the name, phone and address, and CHECKs or NOT NULLs forbid blanking them. Deletion also has to beat every request that could store data for the account while it runs: checkout, offer checkout, a problem report and an address write.

## Decision

1. **Fresh auth, then refusals.** The body is `{current_password}` and nothing else: the slice names the password as the fresh-auth check, so api-endpoints' extra `confirmation` literal is dropped (a confirm step is the frontend's). The password is verified, and the replacement hash computed, before any lock, so no lock waits on Argon2. A wrong password is `401 INVALID_CREDENTIALS`; the hash is compared again under the user lock, so a password changed meanwhile is refused too. An administrator is `409 ACCOUNT_DELETION_BLOCKED`, `reason: administrator` (Q5): an admin account is closed by VS-011 deactivation, which keeps the last-administrator guard. Then, under the locks, `reason: open_order` or `unsettled_refund` (BR-126). CSRF, then the verified check, then 5 attempts an hour per user (Q6).
2. **Orders lose every personal field (Q1).** `orders.anonymized_at` is new. `ck_orders_physical_has_phone`, `ck_orders_delivery_address` and `ck_orders_phone_hash_pair` hold only while it is null. `ck_orders_anonymised_shape` requires `recipient_name`, `contact_phone_snapshot`, `city`, `area`, `street`, `landmark_notes` and `customer_note` null once it is set. `ck_orders_anonymised_closed` refuses it on a `pending_payment` or `in_progress` order. A closed order never reopens, so later refunds and recomputes keep passing. **The phone hash stays (owner decision, money review).** ADR-038 d23's 24-hour cash hold matches a self-cancelled order by phone hash. Without it, a customer could self-cancel, delete, re-register with the same email and get cash again on the same phone. The hash is an HMAC, which SRS §9.8 already lets survive anonymisation. `customer_email_snapshot` becomes the anonymised email. `governorate` stays (coarse, used by analytics), and so does `admin_note`: the administrator wrote it, and **it may contain personal data an administrator typed** (owner decision); the admin docs tell administrators not to put customer contact details there. Lines, payments, refunds, chargebacks, history and STL delivery records are untouched.
3. **Addresses are scrubbed, not only soft-deleted (owner decision).** `addresses.anonymized_at` is new. The four required personal columns become nullable behind `ck_addresses_required_unless_anonymised`, and `ck_addresses_anonymised_shape` requires an anonymised row to be deleted and to hold no name, phone, city, area, street or landmark. `label` and `governorate` stay.
4. **The rest of the account.** The email becomes `deleted+<user id>@invalid`, unique and unregistrable, so the old address can register again. `password_hash` becomes the Argon2 hash of a discarded secret. `is_active` goes false, `anonymized_at` is set, `auth_epoch` goes up by one and every session is revoked; `deleted_at` stays null (Q7). Unused verification and reset tokens are spent. The wishlist is soft-deleted and each product's counter moves down once, in product-id order. Every live offer, an accepted one included, is withdrawn by a `system` round with no notification (Q3). Contact messages with the `user_id` become name "Deleted customer", the anonymised email, no phone and message `[deleted]`, keeping subject, order link, status and times (Q2, owner change). Outbox rows with the `user_id` get the anonymised address; pending ones are abandoned (`failed`, `account_deleted`, payload redacted), so nothing reaches the old address (Q8). Reviews keep their text: no author is ever shown (ADR-044). The cart is emptied. Cash payment blocks stay: they hold only a hash (ADR-038).
   - **Other customer-written text goes too (owner decision, SRS review).** Every customer-actor offer-round message on any of the user's offers (closed ones included) becomes NULL, and so does the `reason` of every status-history row the customer wrote (their cancel note). An administrator's reply and history notes stay, as `admin_note` does. From now on a cancel is audited with `has_note`, never the text. The user's idempotency records are deleted: a stored checkout response holds the delivery address, the rows are retention-managed, and nobody can replay them.
   - **Nothing is sent to the anonymised address.** A later order, refund or chargeback notice still queues an outbox row to `deleted+<id>@invalid`. The outbox stores any `.invalid` recipient as already `failed` (`account_deleted`, payload redacted), so it is never sent.
5. **All or nothing, one audit row.** Everything runs in the request's unit of work; any failure rolls it all back (FR-03). `user.anonymized` carries only counts, never an email or a name.
6. **Lock order: orders, cart, live offers, then users.** The user's `orders` rows `FOR UPDATE` by id come first (ADR-041: a problem report holds an order and then takes `KEY SHARE` on `users`). Next comes the cart `FOR UPDATE`, created if missing (ADR-024 d9's position), so a checkout waiting on it runs after the deletion. Then the live offers by id, which offer checkout locks after inserting its order. Then `users` `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`: `FOR UPDATE` would deadlock with an offer checkout whose order INSERT already holds `KEY SHARE` on the row (a mutation run raised `DeadlockDetectedError`). That is safe because `ix_users_email_live` is partial, so PostgreSQL does not treat `email` as a key column and rewriting it needs no stronger lock. `tests/concurrency/test_account_deletion_races.py` would fail if that ever changed. BR-126 is read only after every lock is held.
7. **A request queued behind the deletion stores nothing.** Each of these reads the account once more after its lock and refuses an anonymised one with `401 AUTH_REQUIRED` (Q4):
   - cart checkout, after the cart lock;
   - offer checkout, after the offer lock (the withdrawn offer already refuses it);
   - a problem report, after the orders lock;
   - address create, where `lock_address_book` re-checks its WHERE on the committed row.

   In offer checkout the withdrawn offer already refuses with `409 INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, so that re-check is a backstop no known race reaches.

   The deletion holds each of those locks until it commits, so the read sees it. A request that got its lock first commits first, and the deletion then sees its open order or anonymises its row.
8. **An open order** is one in `pending_payment` or `in_progress`, or one with any line outside the terminal states (`delivered`, `picked_up`, `refunded`, `cancelled`), because a `partially_refunded` order can still have a line in progress. **An unsettled refund** is ADR-042's obligation: a pending refund, or a failed one not reissued, `late_capture` included.

## Consequences

+ No table keeps the customer's email, phone, name, address, note or message (`test_deletion_strips_every_personal_value_from_every_table` scans every table), while orders, payments, refunds and STL delivery records survive.
+ Deletion never leaves an anonymised account with a new order, problem report or address, and never deadlocks with checkout, offer checkout or a report (race tests in both orders).
- `admin_note` survives and may hold personal data an administrator typed (d2).
- A completed order can still get a VS-031 return or an admin refund after deletion; `test_an_admin_refund_on_an_anonymised_order_still_works` proves the money path. Card refunds work, but a cash refund has no contact left.
- **Accepted money risk (owner decision): chargeback evidence.** Deletion is allowed as soon as no order is open, so a customer who later files a card chargeback has already removed the delivery address and STL email that would prove delivery. BR-126 has no time window, and adding one would change the SRS.
- **Accepted money risk (owner decision): late capture.** After an administrator releases an order whose attempt was `unknown`, a late capture can still land on the anonymised order and owe a refund to an account nobody can reach.
- Not reached by the scrub: provider webhook payloads (real providers may carry the payer's details; only mock payloads exist today), refund reasons, settlement and void notes typed by an administrator, and the `user_agent` and IP hash of revoked sessions until VS-046's purge.
- VS-047's outbox-failure alert must leave out `account_deleted` rows, or every deletion will raise a false alarm.
- With `users` locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, Postgres may grant an address write and the deletion in either order: the deletion's cart INSERT already holds `KEY SHARE` on the row, so it loses its place in the queue. Both outcomes store no personal data, and the race test checks both.
- Accepted (Q9): a wishlist add, a review or an offer submission authenticated just before the commit can still insert one row for the dead account. Those rows hold no contact data, and nobody can sign in to use them. An offer would expire unanswered.
- A message the customer sent as a guest (no `user_id`) and Bring Your Idea requests (ADR-030) are not linked to the account and are not anonymised, the boundary ADR-046 drew.
- Downgrading `0b349a492461` fails once an anonymised order or address exists: the data is gone for good.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Placeholder values that pass the existing CHECKs** (a fake `+961…` phone, "[deleted]" streets): fake data that looks real.
- **Soft-deleting addresses only**, as the slice first said: the rows would keep the name, phone and street.
- **Keeping the snapshots**: fails the slice's own test and SRS §14.5's "anonymised form".
- **`users FOR UPDATE`**: deadlocks against offer checkout (d6).
- **Locking `users` first**, as the slice said: a cycle with a problem report, which holds an order before its `KEY SHARE` on `users` (ADR-041).
